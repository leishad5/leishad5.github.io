Regarding the quality differences between nearest neighbor interpolation and bilinear interpolation, bilinear interpolation yielded better quality overall. Without zooming in, both interpolation methods result in very similar warped images because neighboring pixels at a large scale are close enough in detail for us to not notice harsh interpolations, especially with large pixel resolutions. I had to zoom into thin edges to find the differences between the two interpolation methods, as shown above for my 3 main images.

Because nearest neighbor uses the closest source pixel to fill in any gaps in the projected image, diagonal edges look blocky and have harsh light/dark transitions. We see these blocky artifacts in the skyline's crane, the lake's dock arches, and the hand of the woman at the Globe. in the skyline and the bridge pillars over the lake. The edges in bilinear interpolation are more blended and smooth because of the weighted sourcing it uses for the imperfectly mapped output pixels.

However, bilinear interpolation was always slower than nearest neighbor interpolation. Nearest neighbor needs one lookup per output pixel, while bilinear needs four.

A.4

Blending into a mosaic

I kept the left image as the reference projection and warped the right image.

First, I computed the homography from the right image to the left image using the 8 correspondences and least squares. Then I multiplied each of the right image’s four corners by $H$ in homogeneous coordinates and divided by the third coordinate, which gave the warped corner positions. I inverse warped the right image onto a canvas (larger than both images) with bilinear interpolation and kept a mask of its output pixels.

To blend, I reused my blending code from Project 2 instead of weighted averaging.

The mask is 1 wherever the left image is closer to the middle than the warped right image by using distance transforms of each image's mask. Like in Project 2, I built Laplacian stacks of both images and a Gaussian stack of the mask, then summed the blended levels.

I changed my Project 2 code in two ways:

  • I used cv.filter2D with reflected borders instead of scipy.signal.convolve2d with symmetric borders. The mosaic images are much larger, and convolve2d with a large kernel would take a while. filter2D gives the same result much faster.
  • I used a 61 × 61 kernel with σ = 10 instead of 19 × 19 with σ = 3. The blur has to be more dramatic for a softer blend at this high of a pixel resolution.

Together, this approach worked surprisingly well and there is minimal ghosting, if at all.
