My final image with the Euler sampling is the sharpest, best looking image and looks the most like a real photo. Gaussian blur removes the noise, but not that well, and it does not have any fine details. The one step looks very smoothed (airbrushed) but it doesn't make the picture look like the original that well either. The Euler image looks realistic, but its fine details differ from the original. Euler sampling predicts the velocity at multiple time points and moves towards committing to an image over time steps, which means it won't be as blurry because it's not averaging images the way one step is.

However, according to MSE, one step scores best (0.0109) while the blurring does the worst (0.0263). This is because a realistic but wrong image will score worse with pixel wise comparisons than an image that uses the expected pixel values. Blur scores worst because it removes noise without recovering any structure.

The patterns I observed in the generated outputs are that the model cannot produce a specific number from pure noise, and more epochs do not change this. All the samples look the same, as an averaged blob of pixels across all the numbers that the image could be. There is no clear distinction to what the image is. These patterns may exist because pure noise gives the model no leads on what the number could be, so it has no information about which digit to produce. Since the model tries to minimize MSE, averaging the training data and using the mean is the best it can do (intercept model).

Without the scheduler

To compensate for removing the scheduler, I trained with a constant learning rate of 1e-3, which is lower than the other learning rate (1e-2) used with the scheduler. The only difference here is that the steps taken to update the weights are much smaller. Even so, the 1e-3 run formed clean digits faster, because 1e-2 made the updates overshoot early in training. Had we used the larger learning rate, the loss may have not converged due to oscillation. Thankfully, this worked well because the no scheduler optimization resulted in well-formed numbers. We can see that using 1e-2 was too high a learning rate, and the scheduler helped bring the learning rate down to converge to better images. Starting at a lower learning rate was low enough to keep a steady formation of the digits, so each update is better and closer to the minimal loss.
